MANI'S ROOM / TYPE STUDY

Three ways to set the room.

A standalone study, not a redesign. Compare the same words on deep forest and ink backgrounds. The accent faces are used sparingly; all body copy stays in readable text faces.

On a wide screen, backgrounds sit side by side. On smaller screens, they stack. Fonts load from Google Fonts; if unavailable, serif and sans-serif fallbacks keep the page usable, but the intended comparison requires the web fonts to load.

A — Literary Gothic B — Metropolitan Romantic C — Bookish After Hours

A — Literary Gothic

Cormorant Garamond gives the room names and welcome a literary softness. One UnifrakturMaguntia initial introduces a restrained historical accent. DM Sans keeps navigation and labels clear.

Use: Cormorant Garamond 500: headings; 400: body. UnifrakturMaguntia 400: the first M in the wordmark only. DM Sans 400/500: navigation and labels.

B — Metropolitan Romantic

Bodoni Moda brings a sharper, high-contrast rhythm to the room titles. Cormorant Garamond keeps the supporting text warm. Pinyon Script appears only on “Stay awhile.” as a formal calligraphic gesture.

Use: Bodoni Moda 400: wordmark and room headings. Cormorant Garamond 400: body. Pinyon Script 400: one large welcome phrase. DM Sans 400/500: navigation and labels.

C — Bookish After Hours

IM Fell English, a revival of seventeenth-century printing types, lends the headings a slightly irregular, bookish voice. Libre Franklin draws on the American Franklin Gothic tradition, bringing an urban print sensibility to clear, intimate supporting text.

Use: IM Fell English 400: wordmark and headings. Libre Franklin 400/500: body, navigation, and labels. No decorative accent face; the character comes from the pairing.
